# Quality Metrics and Continuous Improvement

## Mandatory Rules

- **TST-166** — Quality metrics must support decisions and must not reward superficial test volume or coverage inflation.
- **TST-167** — Teams must track meaningful indicators such as escaped defects, defect age, flaky tests, pipeline stability, change failure, recovery time, and risk coverage.
- **TST-168** — Metrics must include context, trend, ownership, and thresholds rather than isolated numbers.
- **TST-169** — Coverage metrics must distinguish code, requirement, risk, platform, and user-journey coverage.
- **TST-170** — Quality dashboards must expose unresolved critical risks and evidence freshness.
- **TST-171** — Test duration and resource cost must be optimized without removing mandatory risk coverage.
- **TST-172** — Repeated manual checks should be evaluated for automation, while low-value automation should be retired.
- **TST-173** — Quality retrospectives must use defects, incidents, user feedback, telemetry, and delivery outcomes.
- **TST-174** — Improvement actions must have owners, priorities, due dates, and verification criteria.
- **TST-175** — Test suites must be periodically reviewed for duplication, obsolete behavior, weak assertions, and missing risk coverage.
- **TST-176** — Quality standards must be updated when technologies, threats, architecture, regulations, or failure patterns change.
- **TST-177** — Benchmark comparisons must use consistent environments, workloads, datasets, and measurement methods.
- **TST-178** — Metrics used for governance must be reproducible and protected against manual manipulation.
- **TST-179** — Teams must distinguish product-quality failures from environment, tooling, and process failures.
- **TST-180** — Continuous improvement must prioritize prevention of high-impact failures over cosmetic metric improvement.
