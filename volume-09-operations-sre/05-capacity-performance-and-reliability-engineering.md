# Capacity, Performance, and Reliability Engineering

- **SRE-091 — Capacity Model:** Critical services MUST maintain a capacity model for primary compute, memory, storage, connection, queue, and network constraints.
- **SRE-092 — Peak Planning:** Capacity planning MUST account for expected peaks, growth, failover scenarios, and maintenance states.
- **SRE-093 — Headroom:** Critical services SHOULD maintain documented operational headroom above normal peak usage.
- **SRE-094 — Saturation Thresholds:** Resource saturation thresholds MUST be defined before exhaustion becomes user impact.
- **SRE-095 — Load Testing:** Material capacity or architecture changes SHOULD be validated under representative load before production.
- **SRE-096 — Stress Testing:** Critical services SHOULD understand behavior beyond expected capacity and identify safe degradation points.
- **SRE-097 — Soak Testing:** Stateful, memory-sensitive, or long-running systems SHOULD be tested for degradation over extended operation.
- **SRE-098 — Queue Monitoring:** Asynchronous systems MUST monitor backlog depth, age, processing rate, failure rate, and dead-letter conditions where applicable.
- **SRE-099 — Connection Exhaustion:** Databases, pools, sockets, and downstream connections MUST have bounded limits and saturation telemetry.
- **SRE-100 — Backpressure:** Systems MUST define how overload is controlled rather than allowing unbounded work accumulation.
- **SRE-101 — Graceful Degradation:** Critical services SHOULD define lower-cost or reduced-function behavior for overload and dependency degradation.
- **SRE-102 — Load Shedding:** Systems MAY reject low-priority work deliberately to preserve critical paths under severe overload.
- **SRE-103 — Rate Limiting:** Rate limits MUST be aligned with capacity, abuse protection, and fair resource allocation.
- **SRE-104 — Timeout Budgets:** Request timeouts SHOULD be budgeted across dependency chains rather than configured independently.
- **SRE-105 — Retry Budgets:** Retries MUST be bounded and MUST NOT amplify overload or dependency failure.
- **SRE-106 — Circuit Breaking:** Repeated dependency failure SHOULD trigger protective isolation where appropriate.
- **SRE-107 — Bulkheads:** Critical resource pools or workflows SHOULD be isolated to prevent one workload from exhausting all capacity.
- **SRE-108 — Cache Dependency:** Cache failure behavior MUST be understood so cache loss does not unexpectedly overload authoritative systems.
- **SRE-109 — Hotspot Detection:** Partition, tenant, key, route, or node hotspots SHOULD be observable for horizontally scaled systems.
- **SRE-110 — Autoscaling Evidence:** Autoscaling thresholds and limits MUST be validated against actual workload and startup characteristics.
- **SRE-111 — Cold Start:** Systems with slow startup, model load, warm cache, or JIT behavior MUST account for cold-start impact in capacity plans.
- **SRE-112 — Performance Regression:** Critical latency and throughput characteristics SHOULD be regression-tested across releases.
- **SRE-113 — Resource Leak Detection:** Long-running services MUST be monitored for memory, handle, connection, thread, and storage leaks where relevant.
- **SRE-114 — Capacity Forecasting:** Material growth trends SHOULD be forecast far enough ahead to avoid emergency scaling.
- **SRE-115 — Capacity Review:** Critical service capacity assumptions MUST be periodically reviewed against actual telemetry and business demand.
